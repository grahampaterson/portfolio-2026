term: Motte and bailey

Borrowed from medieval fortification, by way of philosophy (Nicholas Shackel). An
argument has two parts: a *bailey* — the contested claim you actually want to
hold — and a *motte* — the easily defensible position you retreat to when
challenged.

In design review it sounds like "but what about accessibility?" The motte is the
part nobody will argue with: accessibility matters. The bailey is what's actually
being defended — we shouldn't rebuild the design system, we shouldn't build the
custom control, we shouldn't do the hard version. Push on it and the retreat is
instant (you're not against accessibility, are you?), and the bailey stays
quietly occupied.

**Accessibility-washing** is the same move seen from outside, after
safety-washing and security-washing: a legitimate concern borrowed as cover for a
decision made on other grounds — risk-aversion, or not wanting to do the work.

The tell is what comes next. A real constraint arrives with a next step: here's
who it breaks for, here's what it would take to fix. A motte is satisfied when
the idea goes away.
